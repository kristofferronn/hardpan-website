What kind of game it is

Everyone is someone

Your people are individuals with homes, trades and families. There are no throwaway armies: send men to sea and the fields at home go unharvested.

Everything is carried

There is no global stockpile. Water travels in jugs fired at your kiln, grain in baskets on someone's back. A well placed too far away is a problem you can see from the walking.

The world ends slowly

Droughts come first, then the raiders and the broken trade routes. Your palace economy has to be taken apart in time to save the people inside it.

Where it stands

Collapse is in early development by Hardpan Games, a one-person studio. I design it and make the art myself, and AI tools do much of the coding.

The simulation already runs a grey-box village of 30 people who draw water, farm, dig clay, fell trees, fire new jugs and build houses, with every load carried by hand and paths wearing in where they walk. Next come woodcutters' camps and farms, families with children who grow up, and roads, then the first outside playtests in December, while the final art is made alongside.

A Kickstarter is planned for spring 2027, once a playable piece of the game looks the way it should. Sign up to hear when it goes live.
